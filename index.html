AGX Lab

Affective Game Experience Lab · College of Arts, Chung-Ang University

We seek to explore how emotion and cognition interact within games and interactive media. Grounded in affective game computing and cognitive science, our research aims to understand how affective signals and cognitive processes shape play, decision-making, and multimodal experience through experimental and creative approaches. Alongside empirical studies, the lab takes part in creative production in animation and games with industry and international partners.

research areas

Affective–Cognitive Interaction in Games

Exploring how emotion and cognition interact to shape play, perception, and decision-making in interactive game environments through affective loops and behavioral modeling.

Embodied Interaction & Multimodal Analysis

Investigating embodied and predictive processes in gameplay using multimodal behavioral data, such as player choices, reaction time, and interaction patterns.

Affective Experience and Media Interaction

Exploring how emotional experience is formed and communicated through narrative, animation, and interactive media to understand how affect shapes perception and meaning in digital environments.

  2. JKGS
    An Integrative Analysis of Games and Affect Research: Focusing on Affective Game Computing and Feedback Loop Types
    Hee Seon Kim
    Journal of Korea Game Society, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    This study examines research trends in affective game computing through keyword network analysis and compares three feedback-loop types (cognition-based consequence loops, behavior-based affective loops, and physiological-signal-based affective game loops), analyzing their operating principles, characteristics, and technical limitations to provide an integrated framework for the field.

  3. JKGS
    A Study on Overlapped Configuration and Meta-simulation on Open-world
    Hee Seon Kim
    Journal of Korea Game Society, 2020
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    Explores open-world games through Frasca’s notions of paidia and ludus, examining how open worlds overlap non-linear events with linear quests across a geographic world, and proposes meta-simulation as an alternative modeling tool that expands gameplay possibilities.
